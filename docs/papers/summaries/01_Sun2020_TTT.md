# Sun et al. 2020 — Test-Time Training (TTT)

**Reference**
Sun, Y., Wang, X., Liu, Z., Miller, J., Efros, A., Hardt, M.
*Test-Time Training with Self-Supervision for Generalization under Distribution Shifts.*
ICML 2020. [arXiv:1909.13231](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13231).

Local PDF: [`Test_time_training_with_self_supervision_for_generalization_under_distribution_shifts.pdf`](../pdfs/Test_time_training_with_self_supervision_for_generalization_under_distribution_shifts.pdf)

## Core idea

At test time, ground-truth labels are unavailable, but a **self-supervised** task (here: predicting image rotation among 0°/90°/180°/270°) can still be solved on the test image itself. Taking one or more SGD steps on this auxiliary loss before the prediction adapts the encoder to the new distribution.

## Y-shaped architecture

- **Shared encoder** $f$ feeding two heads:
  - classification head $h_{\text{cls}}$ (10 classes for CIFAR),
  - auxiliary rotation head $h_{\text{rot}}$ (4 classes).
- During training (Stage B.2 in our project): joint loss $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}$.
- At test time: classifier head is frozen, one or more SGD steps are taken on $\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}$ (encoder + rotation head), then classification.

## Two adaptation regimes

- **Per-batch**: one step per test mini-batch — stable, low overhead.
- **Per-image**: one step per single image (using $K$ rotated copies as a synthetic batch) — more precise in theory, but sensitive to gradient noise.

## Original paper results

- ResNet-26 on CIFAR-10-C: several points of average improvement across corruptions, especially at high severities.
- No degradation on clean data — the mechanism does not break the classifier when there is no shift.

## Why this reference for us

This is **the TTT method we implement** (see `src/ttt/adapter.py`) and the first reference in the TER subject. Our code is adapted from the official implementation:
[`yueatsprograms/ttt_cifar_release`](https://github.com/yueatsprograms/ttt_cifar_release).

## Key takeaways for the presentation

- The original paper uses **ResNet + BatchNorm**. We use **ViT + LayerNorm**, which changes the dynamics of TTT (see annexes C and D of our presentation).
- Sun 2020 reports gains in per-image. In our ViT/LN setup, per-image **degrades** — that is the central observation of our project.
